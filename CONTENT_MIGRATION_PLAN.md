### 4.1 Blog Post #1: Corporate Governance

**Title:** Understanding Corporate Governance in Sri Lanka: A Guide for Business Owners

**URL Slug:** `understanding-corporate-governance-sri-lanka`

**Meta Title:** Corporate Governance in Sri Lanka | Austin Law Chambers

**Meta Description:** Understanding Corporate Governance in Sri Lanka: A Guide for Business Owners

**Author:** Austin Law Chambers

**Word Count:** ~700 words

**Full Article:**

Corporate governance is the framework of rules, practices, and processes that guide how a company is directed and controlled in Sri Lanka. With the Companies Act No. 07 of 2007 and recent amendments in 2025, understanding your governance obligations is essential for company directors and shareholders. This guide provides an overview of key corporate governance requirements for Sri Lankan businesses.

**What is Corporate Governance?**

Corporate governance refers to the system by which companies are directed and controlled. It encompasses the relationships between a company's management, board of directors, shareholders, and other stakeholders. Good corporate governance ensures accountability, fairness, and transparency in a company's relationship with all its stakeholders.

Effective governance is crucial for several reasons:
- It protects shareholder interests and investments
- It enhances company reputation and credibility
- It reduces the risk of fraud and mismanagement
- It improves access to capital and reduces cost of financing
- It ensures compliance with legal and regulatory requirements

**Legal Framework in Sri Lanka**

The primary legislation governing corporate governance in Sri Lanka is the Companies Act No. 07 of 2007. This Act sets out the legal framework for company formation, operation, and dissolution. The 2025 amendments to the Companies Act introduced significant changes, including:

- Mandatory disclosure of beneficial ownership details
- Prohibition of bearer shares and share warrants to bearer
- Enhanced transparency requirements for corporate structures
- Stricter penalties for non-compliance

These amendments align Sri Lanka with international best practices and anti-money laundering standards. Companies now have 60 days to disclose beneficial ownership information or risk losing associated rights.

The Registrar of Companies (ROC) oversees company registration and compliance through the eROC (e-Registrar of Companies) online system, making company registration and ongoing compliance more accessible than ever.

**Key Governance Requirements**

**Director Duties and Responsibilities**

Directors owe several fundamental duties to the company:
- **Duty of Care and Diligence:** Directors must exercise reasonable care, skill, and diligence in their role
- **Duty to Act in Good Faith:** All actions must be in the best interests of the company
- **Duty to Avoid Conflicts of Interest:** Directors must disclose any personal interests that conflict with the company's interests

**Board Composition and Meetings**

Private limited companies in Sri Lanka must have at least one director, while public companies typically require at least two directors. For larger companies, having independent directors is considered best practice, especially if the company is listed on the Colombo Stock Exchange.

Board meetings should be held regularly—at a minimum, quarterly. These meetings must be properly documented with minutes recording decisions made and actions to be taken. The board is responsible for strategic oversight, risk management, and ensuring compliance with legal requirements.

**Shareholder Rights and Meetings**

Shareholders have fundamental rights including:
- The right to vote on key company matters
- The right to receive dividends when declared
- The right to access company information
- The right to attend Annual General Meetings (AGMs)

Every company must hold an AGM within 18 months of incorporation and then annually thereafter. AGMs provide a forum for shareholders to receive company reports, ask questions, and vote on important resolutions.

**Company Secretary Requirements**

Under the Companies Act, certain companies must appoint a licensed company secretary. This includes:
- Public companies
- Companies with paid-up share capital exceeding a threshold set by the Registrar
- Subsidiaries of foreign companies

The company secretary plays a crucial role in ensuring compliance with statutory requirements, maintaining company registers, and facilitating communication between the board and shareholders.

**Compliance and Best Practices**

Beyond legal minimums, companies should consider these best practices:

**Regular Compliance Review:** Conduct annual reviews to ensure all statutory filings are up to date. This includes annual returns, financial statements, and changes to company structure.

**Maintain Proper Records:** Keep accurate and up-to-date registers including:
- Register of members (shareholders)
- Register of directors
- Register of beneficial owners (required under 2025 amendments)
- Register of charges

**Internal Controls:** Implement robust internal control systems to prevent fraud, ensure accurate financial reporting, and manage business risks.

**Policies and Procedures:** Develop clear policies on critical areas such as:
- Code of conduct and ethics
- Conflict of interest management
- Whistleblower protection
- Risk management framework

**Conclusion**

Effective corporate governance is not just about legal compliance—it's about building a sustainable, trustworthy business that creates value for all stakeholders. The 2025 amendments to Sri Lanka's Companies Act reflect the government's commitment to higher corporate governance standards and greater transparency.

Whether you're incorporating a new company, restructuring an existing entity, or reviewing your governance framework, our experienced corporate law team can provide the guidance you need.

### 4.2 Blog Post #2: Property Transfer Process

**Title:** The Property Transfer Process in Colombo: What Buyers and Sellers Need to Know

**URL Slug:** `property-transfer-process-colombo`

**Meta Title:** Property Transfer Process in Colombo | Sri Lanka Conveyancing Guide

**Meta Description:** Step-by-step guide to buying and selling property in Colombo. Learn about title searches, documentation, and registration requirements in Sri Lanka.

**Author:** Austin Law Chambers

**Word Count:** ~750 words

**Full Article:**

Buying or selling property in Colombo is a significant financial decision that requires careful legal navigation. The property transfer process in Sri Lanka involves multiple steps, from initial negotiations to final registration at the Land Registry. Understanding this process helps ensure a smooth transaction and protects your interests. This guide outlines the essential steps and considerations for property transfers in Colombo.

**Before the Sale: Due Diligence**

The foundation of any successful property transaction is thorough due diligence. Before committing to a purchase, buyers should undertake several critical checks.

**Title Search and Verification**

The first and most important step is verifying the seller's legal ownership of the property. This involves:
- Conducting a title search at the Registrar General's Department
- Examining the chain of ownership to ensure clear title
- Checking for any encumbrances, mortgages, or liens on the property
- Verifying property boundaries and land extent match official records

Sri Lanka operates under both a deed registration system and a title registration system (under the Registration of Title Act No. 21 of 1998). Properties with registered titles offer greater security, as the state guarantees ownership.

**Physical Inspection**

Beyond legal documentation, buyers should:
- Physically inspect the property for structural issues
- Verify building approvals and compliance with zoning regulations
- Check for environmental concerns or planning restrictions
- Confirm utilities and access rights

**Preliminary Agreement**

Once initial due diligence is satisfactory, parties typically enter into a preliminary agreement or "agreement for sale." This document:
- Sets out the terms and conditions of the sale
- Specifies the purchase price and payment schedule
- Establishes a timeline for completion
- Usually involves a deposit payment (typically 10% of purchase price)

**Legal Documentation**

The formal transfer of property requires precise legal documentation prepared by qualified professionals.

**Deed of Transfer (Conveyance Deed)**

The Deed of Transfer is the primary legal document that transfers ownership. Key points:
- Must be prepared by a notary attorney (also called notary public)
- Must include detailed property description, boundaries, and extent
- Must identify all parties (buyer, seller, witnesses)
- Must be executed in the presence of the notary and two witnesses

**Required Documents**

Parties must provide:
- Original title deed or Certificate of Title
- Tax clearance certificates (proof that property taxes are current)
- National Identity Cards or Passports of all parties
- Survey plans (if required)
- Building approvals (for improved properties)

**Stamp Duty and Taxes**

Property transfers in Sri Lanka attract several taxes:
- **Stamp Duty:** Calculated as a percentage of the property value or consideration (whichever is higher)
- **Capital Gains Tax:** Sellers may be liable depending on how long they've owned the property
- **Municipal Taxes:** All outstanding property taxes must be cleared before transfer

The buyer is typically responsible for stamp duty, while the seller pays capital gains tax. However, parties can negotiate who bears these costs.

**Registration Process**

Once the Deed of Transfer is executed, it must be registered to be legally effective.

**Submitting to the Land Registry**

The registration process involves:
- Lodging the executed deed at the relevant Registrar General's Department office
- Paying registration fees based on the property value
- Submitting all supporting documents
- Processing times vary but typically take several weeks in Colombo

**Title Registration vs. Deed Registration**

Sri Lanka has two systems:
- **Deed Registration:** The traditional system where deeds are simply recorded. The deed itself proves ownership.
- **Title Registration (Registration of Title Act):** The more secure system where the state guarantees ownership through a Certificate of Title.

Properties with registered titles are preferable as they offer greater certainty and protection against fraud. If your property is under the deed registration system, you may consider converting to title registration for enhanced security.

**Final Steps**

After registration:
- Obtain certified copies of the registered deed
- Update municipal records with the new owner's details
- Transfer utility accounts (water, electricity)
- Arrange for physical handover of the property

**Common Issues and How to Avoid Them**

**Defective Titles**

Some common title defects include:
- Breaks in the chain of ownership
- Undisclosed mortgages or liens
- Boundary discrepancies
- Ownership disputes

Always engage an experienced property lawyer to conduct comprehensive title searches and resolve any defects before proceeding.

**Encroachments and Boundary Disputes**

Physical boundaries don't always match legal descriptions. Professional surveys can identify discrepancies early, allowing for resolution before purchase.

**Outstanding Mortgages**

Ensure all existing mortgages are discharged before or at the time of transfer. The purchase price often includes paying off the seller's mortgage, but this must be clearly documented.

**Importance of Legal Representation**

Given the complexity and stakes involved, both buyers and sellers should engage qualified legal representation. A property lawyer will:
- Conduct thorough due diligence
- Identify and resolve potential issues
- Prepare or review all documentation
- Ensure proper registration
- Protect your interests throughout the process

**Conclusion**

The property transfer process in Colombo requires attention to detail, proper legal guidance, and thorough documentation. From conducting comprehensive title searches to ensuring all paperwork is correctly prepared and registered, each step is crucial to a successful transaction.
